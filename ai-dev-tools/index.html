The AI dev tools people actually use.

Not a launch list and not a sponsored roundup. I read what developers said about their own setups in 24 Hacker News threads, then cross-checked it against Lobsters, dev.to, GitHub, npm and PyPI. Here is the result, ranked within each category.

    What the data says

    1. Three harnesses won the terminal.

      In the November 2026 “what’s your setup” thread, 26% of the 538 people answering name Claude Code, 24% Codex and 17% OpenCode. Codex roughly tripled its share of commenters since 2025. Most people run two of them and switch by subscription.

    2. One in nine built their own.

      58 of those 538 describe a harness, agent loop or orchestrator they wrote themselves, more than name Pi, Cursor or Copilot. The harness is becoming what dotfiles were: personal, forked, and shared as a gist.

    3. The IDE is no longer the centre.

      Cursor went from 8.7% of commenters in 2025 threads to 2.2% in the second half of 2026. Windsurf, Continue and Roo Code all but disappear. Zed is the one editor whose share went up.

    4. The most popular orchestrator is tmux.

      94 people in recent threads describe tmux panes plus git worktrees. Of the dedicated tools, Herdr is the breakout, from zero mentions to 41 in a few months.

    5. Cheap plans plus open harnesses is a real lane now.

      OpenRouter went from 0.4% to 3.3% of commenters, and 32 people mention OpenCode Go. The pattern: DeepSeek, GLM or Qwen through a $10 plan, driven by OpenCode or Pi.

    6. GitHub stars stopped meaning much.

      Several 2026 repos sit at 80k to 440k stars with almost no discussion anywhere. cc-switch has 152k stars and three HN comments in a year. So stars count for little here: what people say they use counts for most.

    How this was made

    Sources I scraped

    • Hacker News: Ask HN threads6,323 comments24 threads where developers describe their AI setups, May 2025 to Nov 2026. The core signal: distinct people naming a tool.
    • Hacker News: stories and comments3,229 storiesFront-page pull since Jan 2025, plus each tool’s comment volume over the last 12 months via the Algolia API, typo tolerance off, checked against a 100-comment sample.
    • Lobsters2,979 storiesStory titles from the ai, vibecoding, programming, devops, release and show tags since Jan 2025.
    • dev.to42,357 postsTop posts of the last year across 12 tags. Practitioner blogging, title mentions weighted by reactions.
    • GitHub120 reposStars, last push and licence for every tool with a public repo.
    • npm and PyPImonthly downloadsLast-month downloads from the npm registry and pypistats.

    Sources worth adding

    • Redditnot scrapedr/LocalLLaMA, r/ClaudeAI and r/ChatGPTCoding are where local-model and harness talk is densest. Reddit blocks anonymous scripts, so this needs a registered OAuth app. First thing I’d add.
    • OpenRouter rankingsusage, not opinionA public leaderboard of tokens routed per app. The closest thing to real usage share for open-model harnesses.
    • VS Code Marketplace and Open VSXinstall countsHard numbers for editor extensions (Cline, Roo, Kilo, Continue), which HN underweights.
    • Bluesky and Xbuilder chatterBluesky’s firehose is open and easy to sample. X has the launches but its API is paid.
    • Discord serversneeds consentWhere support questions and complaints actually happen. Private by default: only with a bot the server admins approve.
    • Newsletters and podcastseditorial cross-checkSimon Willison, Latent Space, The Pragmatic Engineer, Interconnects. Low volume, high signal, good for catching what HN misses.
    • Stack Overflow Developer SurveyyearlyHuge sample, slow cadence. Best for the enterprise defaults this page underrates.

    How the score works

    HN devs naming it in Ask HN threads40
    HN comment volume, last 12 months20
    Adoption: GitHub stars or package downloads16
    HN front-page points on stories about it12
    Lobsters stories7
    dev.to reactions5
    Tone of HN comments±6

    Every signal is log-scaled and divided by the best value in its category, so a tool is ranked against its neighbours rather than against Claude Code. Hosted tools with no public repo or package aren’t penalised: their adoption weight is spread across the HN signals instead.

    What to be sceptical of

    Hacker News skews senior, terminal-first and sceptical. Enterprise defaults like Copilot and JetBrains are underrepresented, and tools popular in China or on Reddit get less credit than they deserve.

    Names collide: Pi, Cursor, Muse, Phoenix. Every tool has a context-aware pattern, every comment count was checked against a sample, and comments that turned out to be about something else are discounted. The November thread’s questionnaire named several tools, so quoted question text was stripped before counting.

    Each tool’s HN comments were also read for tone, which moves its score by up to six points. A tool HN uses but complains about still ranks, with the complaint in its write-up.
